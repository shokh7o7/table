Anatomy of a table

Four parts. No screws you can see.

Scroll and watch the table come together. Each part is shaped separately, then joined by hand with glue and oak pegs, the way tables were built a hundred years ago.

  1. 01
    Top

    Four centimetre planks, matched by grain and edge glued.

  2. 02
    Apron

    A hidden frame that keeps the top flat for decades.

  3. 03
    Joints

    Mortise and tenon, locked with a wooden peg.

  4. 04
    Legs

    Tapered on two faces so the table looks lighter.

From log to dinner

How a Halden is made.

  1. 01

    Selecting the boards

    The maker chooses every board by hand and lays the top out like a puzzle of grain.

  2. 02

    Cutting the joints

    Mortises and tenons are cut to a tenth of a millimetre and fitted dry first.

  3. 03

    Shaping and sanding

    Edges are softened by hand plane, then sanded in five grits until silky.

  4. 04

    Oiling and signing

    Three coats of hardwax oil, then the maker signs the underside of the top.

Questions

Before you order.

How long does it take to make my table?

Most tables are ready in four to six weeks. We send photos from the workshop when your top is glued and when it is oiled.

Can I order a custom size?

Yes. Any length from 120 to 320 cm is possible. Use the planner, then tell us your exact size at checkout.

What if the table gets a scratch?

Oiled wood can be repaired at home. Lightly sand the spot and add a little oil. Every order comes with a care kit.

Do you deliver and assemble?

Delivery is free. Two of our team carry the table in, attach the legs and take all packaging away.
